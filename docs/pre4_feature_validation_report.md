## 1. Executive Summary & Critical Sampling-Rate Correction

During the initial PRE-1 and PRE-2 analysis, an empirical discrepancy was observed in the raw export time steps:
- **Damaged recordings:** $\Delta t = 0.0033\text{ s} \implies \approx 303.03\text{ Hz}$
- **Undamaged recordings:** $\Delta t = 0.0030\text{ s} \implies \approx 333.33\text{ Hz}$

### The Root Cause: Software Rounding Artifact
The research paper (*Katam et al., Structures 71, 2025*) specifies that experimental data acquisition was conducted at a uniform rate of **$300\text{ Hz}$** ($1/300 = 0.003333...\text{ s}$). 
When the time deltas were rounded during software export ($0.0033\text{ s}$ in one export batch and $0.0030\text{ s}$ in another), evaluating FFT frequency axes using $1/\text{median}(\Delta t)$ artificially scaled undamaged frequencies by $333.33 / 303.03 = 1.10$ ($+10\%$). This created an artificial "zero-overlap gap" in $f_1$ that was an artifact of the time column, not structural damage.

### The Engineering Fix:
1. **Nominal Sampling Rate (`NOMINAL_FS_HZ = 300.0`):** All spectral analyses (`compute_spectrum`, `extract_modal_frequencies`, and `extract_features`) now evaluate at the authoritative physical rate of **$300.0\text{ Hz}$**.
2. **Metadata Isolation:** `calculate_sampling_rate()` is preserved for reporting only, stored as metadata (`sampling_rate_hz`), and strictly excluded from model feature sets.
3. **Rate-Independent Modal Ratios:** Added invariant modal ratios `f2_over_f1` ($f_2 / f_1$) and `f3_over_f1` ($f_3 / f_1$), which remain stable regardless of time-axis scaling.

Evaluating recordings at the nominal $300\text{ Hz}$ physical rate aligns the healthy frequencies directly with the published experimental benchmarks:

| **Mode 2 ($f_2$)** | $\approx 37.79\text{ Hz}$ | $\mathbf{37.935 \pm 0.291\text{ Hz}}$ | $39.708 \pm 0.778\text{ Hz}$ | $\mathbf{40.316 \pm 0.877\text{ Hz}}$ |
| **Ratio ($f_2 / f_1$)** | $\approx 4.91$ | $\mathbf{4.986 \pm 0.060}$ | $\mathbf{5.202 \pm 0.105}$ | $\mathbf{5.380 \pm 0.121}$ |

### Key Observations:
1. **Benchmark Alignment:** Healthy $f_1$ ($7.609\text{ Hz}$) and $f_2$ ($37.935\text{ Hz}$) now closely match the paper’s experimental values ($7.69\text{ Hz}$ and $37.79\text{ Hz}$).
2. **Frequency Overlap & Location Dependence:** $f_1$ alone does **not** separate healthy from damaged with zero overlap: for minor $1\text{ mm}$ cracks located near the free end (e.g. M6/M7), the first bending mode curvature is minimal and $f_1$ remains close to $\approx 7.6\text{ Hz}$.
3. **The Invariant Ratio ($f_2 / f_1$):** While individual frequencies shift subtly, the modal ratio $f_2 / f_1$ exhibits a progressive, monotonic shift with damage:
   * Healthy: $4.986 \pm 0.060$ (Range: $4.89 - 5.09$)
   * Damaged 1mm: $5.202 \pm 0.105$ (Range: $5.12 - 6.05$)
   * Damaged 2mm: $5.380 \pm 0.121$ (Range: $5.15 - 5.55$)
   This ratio provides a rate-independent feature for classification and quantification.

## 4. Interface A Schema Definition

The feature table generated by `extract_features()` strictly separates metadata from training features:

### A. Metadata (Excluded from Model Input Features)
- `recording_id` (str): Unique file stem.
- `case_id` (str): Group identifier for Leave-One-Case-Out cross-validation (e.g. `1mm_M1`, `2mm_M3`, `Undamaged_1st`).
- `source_file` (str): Original file path.
- `specimen` (str): Specimen tag (`M1`–`M7`, `1st`, `2nd`).
- `test_type` (str): Excitation method (`Displacement`, `Singlehit`, `Multihit`, `Randomhit`).
- `sampling_rate_hz` (float): Measured empirical export rate ($\approx 303.03$ or $\approx 333.33\text{ Hz}$). Strictly excluded from model training.

### B. Labels (Training Targets Only)
- `condition` (str): `Damaged` vs `Undamaged`.
- `damage_level_mm` (float): `1.0`, `2.0`, `3.0` (or `NaN` for Undamaged).

### C. Input Features (40 Features)
- Natural Frequencies: `f1_hz`, `f2_hz`, `f3_hz` (evaluated at $300\text{ Hz}$)
- Modal Ratios: `f2_over_f1`, `f3_over_f1`
- Per-Sensor Time Domain Metrics (`Sensor_1` to `Sensor_5`):
  - RMS: `Sensor_{i}_rms`
  - Kurtosis: `Sensor_{i}_kurtosis`
  - Skewness: `Sensor_{i}_skewness`
  - Variance: `Sensor_{i}_variance`
  - Peak Amplitude: `Sensor_{i}_peak`
  - Crest Factor: `Sensor_{i}_crest_factor`
  - Peak-to-Peak: `Sensor_{i}_p2p`

## 7. PRE-4 Peak-Picking & Search-Band Refinement Audit (Varshneya Sign-Off Item)

### 7.1 Diagnostic Audit & Root-Cause Identification
Following the PRE-4 review request by Varshneya, an audit of `features_dataset.csv` was conducted using a robust MAD z-score check ($z > 4$) across all cases. This revealed two distinct non-physical spectral anomalies under the initial wide search bands (`f1: 5.5-10 Hz`, `f2: 32-46 Hz`, `f3: 115-150 Hz`):

1. **Nyquist Boundary Artifact in Mode 3 ($f_3$):**
   * *Observation:* In case `2mm_M2`, 10 out of 20 recordings reported $f_3$ at $148.5 - 149.8\text{ Hz}$, inflating the case mean $f_3$ to $141.1\text{ Hz}$ (whereas neighbouring cases were $\approx 125 - 133\text{ Hz}$) and producing 4 picks right on the upper band edge ($150.0\text{ Hz}$).
   * *Root Cause:* At $f_s = 300\text{ Hz}$, the Nyquist frequency is exactly $f_{Nyq} = 150.0\text{ Hz}$. When Mode 3 excitation amplitude was small, the peak picker selected high-frequency anti-aliasing filter skirt noise at the Nyquist edge.
2. **Fixture / Impact Transient Artifact in Mode 2 ($f_2$):**
   * *Observation:* In case `1mm_M5`, single-hit impulse recordings (`D-SM_1-2`, `D-SM_2-3`) yielded $f_2 = 45.97\text{ Hz}$, pushing the case $f_2$ standard deviation to $2.03\text{ Hz}$ and hitting the upper band edge ($46.0\text{ Hz}$).
   * *Root Cause:* The $45.97\text{ Hz}$ peak corresponds to impact hammer / fixture contact dynamics. Mode 2 bending vibration for the beam is located at $36.8 - 41.5\text{ Hz}$.

### 7.2 Physical Search-Band Refinements
The search bands were tightened strictly based on Euler-Bernoulli cantilever modal physics and published benchmarks from *Katam et al., Structures 71 (2025)*:

| Frequency Mode | Previous Band | Refined Physical Band | Physical Justification |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Mode 1 ($f_1$)** | $5.5 - 10.0\text{ Hz}$ | $\mathbf{6.5 - 8.8\text{ Hz}}$ | Encompasses all damaged ($7.0 - 7.6\text{ Hz}$) and healthy ($7.69\text{ Hz}$) experimental modes while rejecting low-frequency drift ($< 6\text{ Hz}$). |
| **Mode 2 ($f_2$)** | $32.0 - 46.0\text{ Hz}$ | $\mathbf{36.0 - 43.5\text{ Hz}}$ | Safely encompasses all experimental Mode 2 bending vibrations ($36.8 - 41.5\text{ Hz}$) while strictly cutting off fixture contact transients near $46\text{ Hz}$ and subharmonic drift near $33\text{ Hz}$. |
| **Mode 3 ($f_3$)** | $115.0 - 150.0\text{ Hz}$ | $\mathbf{120.0 - 142.0\text{ Hz}}$ | Encompasses the full physical range ($124.0 - 138.0\text{ Hz}$) while eliminating the Nyquist skirt region ($142 - 150\text{ Hz}$, $f_{Nyq} = 150\text{ Hz}$). |

> **Strict Non-Tuning Guarantee:** No damage labels, file names, or case names are used in peak selection. The picker remains completely unsupervised and physics-grounded.

### 7.3 Quantitative Results & Impact
Following the band update, `features_dataset.csv` and `healthy_baseline.json` were regenerated:

* **Band-Edge Hits:** Dropped from 10 recordings sitting within $0.1\text{ Hz}$ of the boundary to **0** (zero edge artifacts across all modes).
* **Case `2mm_M2`:**
  * $f_2$ standard deviation: Dropped from $1.705\text{ Hz} \to \mathbf{0.148\text{ Hz}}$.
  * $f_3$ mean: Normalized from $141.1\text{ Hz} \to \mathbf{125.69\text{ Hz}}$ (now aligning with neighbouring case `2mm_M1` at $125.87\text{ Hz}$ and `Undamaged_1st` at $125.95\text{ Hz}$).
  * $f_3$ standard deviation: Dropped from $12.27\text{ Hz} \to \mathbf{3.02\text{ Hz}}$.
* **Case `1mm_M5`:**
  * $f_2$ standard deviation: Dropped from $2.03\text{ Hz} \to \mathbf{0.804\text{ Hz}}$.
  * Detailed inspection shows 18 of 20 recordings cluster tightly between $38.83$ and $39.65\text{ Hz}$ ($\text{median} = 39.35\text{ Hz}$). The remaining variance arises from genuine physical modal excitation differences: single hits at clamp-proximal segments excite a localized vibration mode at $36.8\text{ Hz}$ rather than $39.4\text{ Hz}$.
* **Total Suspect Rows ($z > 4$):** Dropped by $51\%$ (from 61 down to 30). The remaining 30 flags reflect genuine physical differences between static displacement releases (which excite high Mode 3 participation near $136\text{ Hz}$) and dynamic impulse hammer hits (which settle near $127\text{ Hz}$).

### 7.4 Assumptions, Limitations & Sampling Rate Specifications
1. **Empirical Selection on Dataset:** The search bands were determined after inspecting the spectral distributions of this dataset, bounded by cantilever beam physics.
2. **Search-Band Boundary Limitation:** Any modal frequency falling outside the hard search bands will be missed. For instance, in a different specimen or future configuration where $f_2 > 43.5\text{ Hz}$, the picker would not locate the true peak and would instead pick the highest local value within $(36.0, 43.5)\text{ Hz}$.
3. **Sampling Rates and Nyquist Frequencies:**
   - **FFT Evaluation Rate:** All frequency spectra and peak picking evaluate at `NOMINAL_FS_HZ = 300.0 Hz` uniformly across all recordings to eliminate the 10% export rounding bias between undamaged and damaged batches. Under this nominal grid, the FFT frequency axis cuts off at $f_{Nyq} = 300.0 / 2 = 150.0\text{ Hz}$.
   - **File-Specific Measured Rates:** For metadata auditing only, `calculate_sampling_rate()` computes the empirical rate from each file's time delta. Damaged recordings measure $\approx 303.03\text{ Hz}$ ($\Delta t = 0.0033\text{ s}$, physical $f_{Nyq} \approx 151.5\text{ Hz}$), while undamaged recordings measure $\approx 333.33\text{ Hz}$ ($\Delta t = 0.0030\text{ s}$, physical $f_{Nyq} \approx 166.7\text{ Hz}$).
   - The tightened upper bound for Mode 3 ($142.0\text{ Hz}$) sits cleanly below both the $150.0\text{ Hz}$ nominal FFT limit and the file-specific empirical Nyquist frequencies ($151.5\text{ Hz}$ and $166.7\text{ Hz}$), preventing roll-off filter skirt artifacts.
